# HFT Project

A *Rust–Python* project aiming to build the software infrastructure for **industrial algorithmic trading**, covering both research and execution. The project explores *HFT-inspired execution infrastructure* alongside automated data pipelines and *machine learning approaches* for trading decisions and financial market modelling.

## Overview

The design is guided by an end-to-end view of the algorithmic trading lifecycle. Inspired by Marcos López de Prado's *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, the following pipeline serves as a conceptual framework for the project's architectural decisions.

| Stage | Process                                      | Purpose                                            |
| :---: | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
|   01  | **Data collection & curation**               | Build reliable, research-ready datasets            |
|   02  | **Feature engineering & pattern discovery**  | Identify informative features and market patterns  |
|   03  | **Strategy development & formalisation**     | Translate research into explicit trading logic     |
|   04  | **Validation & risk assessment**             | Evaluate robustness, performance, and risk         |
|   05  | **Deployment & execution**                   | Implement strategies in a live trading environment |
|   06  | **Portfolio monitoring & trading oversight** | Monitor positions, performance, and live behaviour |

The architecture is intended to reflect this lifecycle, with an emphasis on modularity, reproducibility, and automation. Each stage should be able to operate independently while remaining integrated into a coherent research-to-production workflow.

## Architecture

The architecture is considered through three conceptual domains: **Data**, **Trading**, and **Operations**. These are not intended to represent strict software boundaries or directory structures, but rather provide a framework for thinking about the different concerns involved in building an industrial algorithmic trading infrastructure. They help guide architectural decisions, identify dependencies, and maintain a coherent design across the different stages of the trading lifecycle. In practice, components may span multiple domains or interact closely with one another.

### Data

The data layer is designed around the different stages and purposes of data encountered throughout the trading lifecycle.

A distinction is made between three stages of data processing:

- **Raw data:** Data collected directly from external sources, potentially in heterogeneous formats and structures. It is initially held in temporary storage until it can be processed, validated, and integrated into the structured data system.
- **Curated data:** Raw data that has undergone initial cleaning, validation, and structuring. It constitutes the reliable, persistent foundation upon which further research and processing can be built.
- **Transformed data:** Data derived from curated datasets through various processing and transformation steps, with the objective of extracting meaningful features, relationships, and market patterns.

A particular emphasis is placed on **temporal integrity**. Given the risks of data leakage and look-ahead bias in quantitative research, structured datasets must follow strict timestamp and data-availability conventions. In particular, it is important to distinguish between event time (when an event occurred), observation time (when it was received by the system), and availability time (when it became accessible to a strategy or research process).

This naturally favours relational and tabular storage models, although other approaches, such as graph-based storage, may be considered where appropriate and with suitable precautions.

Beyond market data, the infrastructure also needs to account for several categories of operational data:

- **Trading data:** Orders, executions, positions, portfolio states, and other information generated by trading activity. This data must be persistent, reliable, and traceable to support auditing, reconciliation, and live monitoring.
- **System data:** Logs, telemetry, configuration history, version control information, and other data related to the operation and maintenance of the infrastructure.
- **Research data:** Backtest results, experiment configurations, model outputs, performance metrics, and other information required to reproduce and compare research processes.

These distinctions guide the choice of storage technologies, database structures, and data management policies. The broader objective is to establish a coherent data infrastructure that minimizes friction between research and production while preserving reliability, traceability, and reproducibility.

### Trading

The Trading domain encompasses the components responsible for representing, simulating, evaluating, and ultimately operating trading activity. The objective is to develop reusable and composable building blocks that can support both research environments and live trading systems.

- **Market representation and simulation:** Components responsible for modelling market behaviour and trading mechanics, including order books, matching engines, market simulators, and backtesting infrastructure.
- **Research and strategy development:** Components supporting quantitative research, feature engineering, statistical analysis, strategy development, and model validation. Particular attention is given to reproducibility, dataset versioning, and experiment tracking.
- **Trading operations and decision-making:** Components responsible for the management of trading activity, including order management, risk calculations, position management, portfolio optimisation, and strategy execution.

A central design consideration is to maintain consistency between research, simulation, and live execution. Components should remain sufficiently decoupled to allow independent development and testing, while sharing common domain definitions and trading abstractions wherever possible.

### Operations

The Operations domain encompasses the components required to run, connect, and supervise the broader information system. The objective is to provide a reliable operational foundation upon which the trading components can run, while keeping infrastructure concerns separate from the underlying trading logic.

- **Telemetry and observability:** Logging, monitoring, metrics collection, and system diagnostics.
- **External connectivity:** Interfaces to market data providers, brokers, exchanges, and other external services.
- **Execution infrastructure:** Processes and services responsible for running trading applications and coordinating their interactions.
- **System management:** Configuration, lifecycle management, error handling, and operational supervision.

## Repository Structure

The conceptual domains above guide the architecture but do not map directly to the codebase. Development currently focuses on foundational Rust libraries and abstractions. Python-based research tooling and the broader data infrastructure remain future development objectives.

The Rust workspace is currently organized as follows:

```text
.
├── crates/           # Reusable Rust libraries
│   ├── telemetry/    # Scope-based logging and monitoring
│   ├── orderbook/    # Order book data structures
│   ├── matching/     # Matching engine implementations
│   └── types/        # Shared domain definitions
│
├── bin/              # Executable applications
├── migrations/       # Database schema and migrations
├── docs/             # Repository documentation
└── ...
```
